## Summary (plain language)
A CLIP ViT-L/14 feature extractor plus a linear SVM, trained on only 1,000-10,000 real/fake pairs from a single diffusion generator (Latent Diffusion with COCO captions), generalizes to GANs, diffusion models and commercial tools (DALL-E 3, Midjourney v5, Firefly) and survives crop/resize/JPEG much better than low-level detectors. Adding resized/compressed images to the small training set improves robustness further.

## How it works
- Features: output of the **next-to-last layer** of CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder (chosen over last layer from preliminary experiments in the supplement), then a linear SVM (chosen among simple classifiers) (Sec. 4).
- Real and fake training images are paired by caption (real COCO image + caption -> text-to-image generator) to avoid semantic bias.
- Released code (read from `main.py`/`config.yaml`): arch `opencliplinearnext_clipL14commonpool` (open_clip ViT-L/14 pre-trained on CommonPool), `patch_size: Clip224`, which applies `Resize(224, bicubic)` in the transform (CenterCrop also imported) -> the released detector **resizes** at test time, unlike UnivFD/RINE. Paper-side test resolution handling: not extracted -> **[unverified]**. Output is an LLR score; LLR>0 = synthetic.
- Paper notes pre-training data scale matters (LAION-400M vs LAION-2B vs CommonPool-12.8B comparison, Fig. on pre-training; exact numbers not extracted -> **[unverified]**).

## Training data
N = 1k or 10k real (COCO) + N fake (Latent Diffusion with COCO captions); "+" variants include compressed/resized images in the reference set (Table 3). Down to 10+10 images still works at reduced quality (Sec. 4.1).

## Reported results
Test: 18 generators over GAN family, diffusion family and commercial tools (Table 1), real counterparts matched per generator.
- AUC, original images (Table 4 AVG): Ours 10k 89.8, 10k+ 90.0, 1k 88.4, 1k+ 87.8; Ojha et al. 84.1; Corvi et al. 84.2; NPR 76.8; Liu et al. 74.3; Wang et al. 64.6. Text claims +6.8% avg AUC over best competitor for 10k; the table difference to Corvi is 5.6 points (wording/averaging mismatch; not resolved).
- AUC, post-processed (random crop, resize to 200x200, JPEG QF 65-100; Table 5 AVG): Ours 10k 78.1, 10k+ 85.2, 1k+ 83.2; Ojha 71.8; Corvi 70.8; Wang 61.5; NPR 51.0; LGrad 49.4. Text: most SoTA near chance on commercial tools after processing; abstract: +13% robustness.
- Accuracy uses fixed threshold 0.5 (Sec. 3); accuracy was lower than AUC and did not always improve with N (Fig. 3).
- Fusion with Corvi low-level detector gives +3.6 AUC, +7.4 accuracy (Table 7 text) on original images; smaller gain on processed.
- Attack test (Table 6, 1k real + 1k fake): removing low-level traces from SDXL images (decimation) or adding them to real images (autoencoder pass) degrades Corvi dramatically but CLIP detector only slightly (numbers not extracted -> **[unverified]**).
- No FPR on fresh real photos.

## Known weaknesses / failure modes
- From paper: accuracy at fixed threshold is far below AUC (Fig. 3); weaker with 10+10 images and low-quality data; AUC on commercial tools lower without resize/JPEG augmentation in the reference set; authors list interpretability and few-shot on-the-fly adaptation as open (Conclusion).
- Reference set is generated by a single latent diffusion model; biases of that dataset (COCO real images are JPEG; fakes PNG?) are not discussed in extracted text -> **[unverified]**, check for format bias.
- Later papers: not found among those checked (B-Free fetch did not evaluate this method; Community Forensics and AIDE fetches did not report it).

## Relevance to this project
- Strongest evidence that tiny, well-chosen training sets + frozen CLIP (penultimate layer) transfer to commercial generators and survive crop/resize/JPEG; closest to our in-the-wild goal.
- Training with resized/compressed samples ("+") trades a little clean AUC for +7 AUC robustness (Table 5, 85.2 vs 78.1).
- Orthogonal to low-level detectors (fusion helps) - useful for an ensemble.
